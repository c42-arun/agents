I strongly support banning the sale of portable barbeques during a heat wave.

The case is simple: heat waves create exactly the conditions in which portable barbeques become a serious fire risk. Dry grass, high temperatures, and often windy conditions turn one careless spark into a devastating wildfire. Portable barbeques are easy to move, easy to misuse, and often used in parks, gardens, campsites, and balconies—places where a small mistake can spread fast.

This is a sensible, temporary precaution, not an attack on personal freedom. People can still cook in other safer ways. But when the danger to public safety, property, wildlife, and emergency services is so high, the government has a duty to act before disaster happens—not after.

We already accept temporary restrictions during extreme weather for roads, water use, and public safety. A heat wave is exactly the time to reduce preventable ignition sources. Banning sales of portable barbeques is a practical, targeted measure that can save homes, protect lives, and help prevent avoidable wildfires.

In short: when the risk of catastrophic fire is high, selling portable barbeques is reckless. The responsible choice is to ban them during a heat wave.
